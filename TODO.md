# FS1R.emu TODO

Goal: a virtual instrument (VST3/CLAP plus standalone) whose GUI is the FS1R front panel, a per-synth engine library with a `synthLib::Device`-style interface (process a block of audio, take MIDI in, give MIDI out, get/set state as sysex), a console test harness on top of it, and a JUCE plugin layer that never models synthesis. The plugin only moves parameter values in and out of the engine as MIDI, exactly as a hardware editor would, and draws a skin bound to a JSON parameter description list.

We rewrite the FS1R's control logic in C++ from the firmware and model the two custom chips, because nobody has register semantics for the YMP706 or an instruction set for the YSS236. See Tier 4 for where processor emulation still fits.

Tiers 0 to 3 and 5 are done, and the CPU-side spots that were read from the firmware but not certain have now been settled from the disassembly (Tier 0, "confirm what was assumed"). What is left is in Tier 4, and nearly all of it waits on hardware or on research nobody has finished: recordings of a real unit to calibrate the chip models against, an SH-2 core to verify the rewrite, and a decode of the VOP3 instruction set. rgwan's answers about the board (2026-09-15 and 2026-09-16) closed the last two firmware-only items: the filter is VOP3-1's and the CPU's whole side of it is read, and the CS2 decode led to the second VOP3's driver, which shows that the microcode extracted so far is the filter's rather than the effects'. Tier 3's remaining line is artwork, not code.

## What we know

The scope below rests on three lists. KNOWN is read from the firmware, its tables or the board. INFERRED is modelled from the DX7 lineage, the formant patent and the Data List, and gathered in `namespace cal` in `src/fs1r_lib.cpp`. UNKNOWN is what no file or photo tells us.

### KNOWN

- **Board.** SH7044 (HD64F7044F28, SH-2, 28 MHz, 256 KB internal flash) with a 2 MB M27C160 EPROM, 512 KB DRAM at 0x01000000, a 1 Mbit battery-backed NVRAM on CS1 (0x400000, user banks). Two YMP706-F tone generators at 0xC00000 and 0xC00400 (16 channels each, 8-bit write-only bus, PBUSY handshake). Two YSS236-F DSPs on CS2 with a 16-bit register block each, VOP3-2 (effects) at 0x800000 and VOP3-1 (the per-voice filter) at 0x800200, status at 0x800242. LED and LCD-contrast latch at 0x800100. Two Sanyo LC78834M 18-bit stereo DACs. Sources: the studiorepair.com FS1R gallery, rgwan's dumps, the firmware's address usage.
- **What is not on the bus.** 0x0140xxxx was listed for weeks as a second external device or NVRAM and is neither: the NVRAM is the 1 Mbit part on CS1, and 0x01000000-0x01FFFFFF is the SH7044's own DRAM area (rgwan, 2026-09-16). No code references the address, the EPROM words that read like pointers to it form no table, and anything that did reach it would alias onto 0x0100xxxx. `docs/research.md` 2.1.
- **Clocks and audio path** (rgwan, 2026-09-15, from the service manual and his board). One 24.576 MHz crystal runs the audio side: the main DAC is the I2S master at 512 Fs, exactly 48 kHz, and clocks the slave DAC, both VOP3s and both YMP706s. The CPU has its own 7 MHz crystal with the PLL at 4x, so the 28 MHz the SCI divisor implied is confirmed and the tick is not locked to the sample clock. FS1A's dry and send busses feed FS1B, which sums both chips and feeds VOP3-2; VOP3-2's SDO1 drives the main DAC and SDO3 the slave DAC, which is the INDIVIDUAL OUTPUT pair. VOP3-1 sits in a channel-level loop off both tone generators. The master volume is an analogue pot after the main DAC. Diagram and consequences in `docs/research.md` 2.0.1.
- **CS2 decode** (rgwan, 2026-09-16, from the schematic). Partial decoding on A8 and A9 alone: `VOP3_SELN = CS2N | A8`, `VOP3-1N = ~A9 | VOP3_SELN`, `VOP3-2N = A9 | VOP3_SELN`, `IC25_26_CLK = ~A8 | CS2N | WRL`. So VOP3-2 sits at 0x800000, VOP3-1 at 0x800200 and the 74HC374 pair at 0x800100, where IC26 drives LED0-7 off the low byte and IC25 drives CONTA/CONTB/CONTC off D8-10 into IC29, the LCD contrast DAC. Nothing above A9 is decoded, so each repeats every 0x400. IC31's WA/WD pins are open, so VOP3-1 has no external DRAM and cannot run a reverb. The firmware has a separate driver for each chip, `FUN_0000B5E2` and `FUN_00039648`; `docs/research.md` section 8.
- **The two VOP3s.** The YSS236 is Yamaha's "VOP3", the same programmable DSP that is the synthesis engine of the AN1x, AN200 and PLG150-AN and the vocal harmony processor of the PSR-9000. The FS1R has two, each with its own driver in the firmware and its own program uploaded at boot: VOP3-1 the filter at 0x800200 (FUN_0000BC8C, extracted into `docs/vop3_microcode.md` and `docs/vop3/` with the register each word goes to) and VOP3-2 the effects at 0x800000 (FUN_0003A064 and FUN_0003C9C4, images at EPROM 0x372204 and 0x373604, not extracted yet). Both programs live in the EPROM, not in the chips.
- **Engine, CPU side.** All 88 algorithms, the DX7 conversion, frequency words, EG rate and level conversion, velocity curves and attenuation, level key scaling, EG bias, note shifts, part detune, master tune, bend, the software pitch EG, LFO1 and LFO2, portamento, mono modes with priorities and legato, note and velocity limits, part volume/expression/balance, pan, performances, Fseq frame timing and playback. The 192.3 Hz tick (MTU2), the Fseq frame timer (CMT1), the YMP706 register map and every conversion table. See `docs/ymp706_registers.md`, and, since 2026-09-18, the voice image checked byte for byte against a running unit.
- **Controller sets.** The whole matrix, read out of FUN_00014DDC, FUN_00014AD0 and FUN_000191C0: the source bit order, the bipolar and raw source conversions, the clamped sum, the three scalers and which of the 48 destinations uses each, the per-part gate, and the destinations that are not a plain offset (pitch bias, amplitude EG bias, frequency bias, bandwidth, the LFO depths, Fseq speed, formant scratch). `docs/ymp706_registers.md` has the tables; `tools/ghidra_ctrl_dests.py` regenerates the handler decompilations.
- **Data.** Voice (608 bytes), performance (400), Fseq and system bulk layouts, the whole sysex parameter map, the 1408 preset voices, 384 performances and 90 Fseqs in the EPROM, the DX7 VCED and ACED maps.
- **Effect parameter encoding.** Read back out of the preset performances and checked against every documented default: frequency index `20 * 2^(i/6)` Hz, gain `v - 64` dB, Q `v/10`, delay words in 0.1 ms, threshold `v - 127` dB, the XG LFO frequency and reverb time curves.

### INFERRED

Every chip-side constant is now in one place, `namespace cal` in `src/fs1r_lib.cpp`, so calibrating against a recording is a table edit.

- YMP706 EG shape and rate scaling. Measured, 2026-09-19, off four envelope recordings: the rate law, the 1.5 dB level step and the 1.5 dB carrier correction step all stand, and the 8-bit level register is 0.376287 dB, a halving every sixteen steps. The attack's shape, its floor, the hold's length and the key rate scaling were all wrong and are now measured. `docs/aeg.md` is the working. What is left is the key code law between its ten measured points, three semitones apart, and the hold's fixed 8 ms lag, whose six-millisecond scatter is the 192.3 Hz tick rather than the chip. Both want register 0xC0 driven directly, which is `FS1R.unlock/docs/unknowns.md` experiment 8.
- Per-op pitch and frequency modulation sensitivity scaling; feedback `0.5 * 2^(fb - 7)`. The modulation index is measured now, 3.369 cycles at full level, off two sideband sweeps and confirmed by `03_fm`'s own spectra. Amplitude sensitivity is measured now and disagrees with the model; detune was measured and disagreed too, and carries the measured curve since 2026-09-20. See `docs/capture_0918.md`.
- Frequency EG range and timing.
- The formant window (bandwidth and skirt), the harmonic forms `all/odd/res`, the noise formant chain. Patent model, see `docs/research.md` section 4.
- The per-voice filter's response. The CPU side is read from the firmware, including the cutoff and both resonance conversions, so what is left is how VOP3-1 reads a coefficient. `cal` takes the cutoff coefficient as a one-pole and runs the three lowpasses as one 4-pole ladder, with resonance table A scaling the feedback (`LADDER_K`) and table B available for passband compensation (`RESO_COMP`, currently 0). HPF, BPF and BEF are separate chip modes and keep the 2-pole SVF reading. The effect algorithms are still modelled from the Data List.

### UNKNOWN

- **YMP706.** Which write starts a note (0xFC/FD is written before the registers load) and the exact response to every register above. No register documentation and no die shot; the chip is too rare for one. The pan path came off this list when FUN_00025BC8 and its siblings were read out, and the filter came off it for good: the firmware sends every filter parameter to VOP3-1 over the 0x800200 block, so this chip only switches the channel loop out to it.
- **YSS236 / VOP3.** The instruction set. VOP3-1's program and coefficient tables are extracted into `docs/vop3/`, but nobody has decoded the format, here or anywhere else, and it is a research project on the scale of a full DSP core. Three things about it are new: the pinout is read, `docs/vop3_pinout.md` out of the AN200 service manual, which bounds the machine at 128 CPU registers, 8 serial inputs and 8 outputs and a bank of external DRAM per chip; the addressing is settled, so the upload already extracted is the filter chip's; and a filter is a smaller and more predictable thing to decode first than a reverb.
- **Firmware odds and ends.** What distinguishes VOP3-1's two program variants, since FUN_0000B010 loads variant 0 on the normal boot and FUN_003C2038, the diagnostic entry, loads variant 1 alongside VOP3-2's image 0; how the filter EG reaches the chip, since the cutoff conversion is only called when a parameter changes. The 0x650000 access in the SCI transmit path is no longer on this list: rgwan confirmed it on hardware on 2026-09-19 as the NVRAM at offset 0x10000, aliased — the 1 Mbit part decodes A0-A16, and 0x650000 - 0x400000 = 0x250000 whose low 17 bits are 0x10000, so a poke at 0x410000 reads back at 0x650000. It is the second user bank, not a device.
- **Hardware behaviour.** Two register sessions off a real unit (2026-09-18 and 2026-09-19) and a recording of the capture set, all in `../FS1R.unlock/captures`. The CPU side of the note-on path is checked byte for byte, 240,476 checks over 802 segments with no mismatch, so what is left under this heading is the chip side: how VOP3-1 reads a filter coefficient, and what the effect blocks do. There is also rgwan's digital recording of the built-in demo, `captures/FS1R DEMO.flac`, which the unit plays from its own EPROM. That fixes the timing and catches gross errors but measures nothing on its own, because the demo never holds a note still. rgwan owns the unit and the digital-out board. The tap is known now: the main DAC's I2S input, after both effect DSPs and before the analogue volume pot, so a recording's absolute level is comparable and the effect blocks are always in the path.

- [x] **Fseq gaps.** Scratch mode from controller destination 47, MIDI clock sync, oneway and round loop modes with the direction the loop points imply, the start offset, the performance start delay, bulk load and the voice Fseq switches.
- [x] **Remaining MIDI.** RPN 0-2, the ten NRPN 01 xx part parameters, bank select and program change in both modes, dump requests answered with checksummed bulk dumps, parameter requests answered, the full 76-byte system block, MIDI clock, active sensing, mono/poly.
- [x] **Voice edit coverage.** Every address in tables 1 to 4 is writable as a single parameter change, including the 112 effect bytes and the Fseq header. The voice's Formant and FM control matrix was the last unimplemented block. `fs1r_emu -selftest` checks each address class.
- [x] **Find where the filter parameters are written.** Traced. The filter is VOP3-1's and the CPU sends it coefficients over the 0x800200 block: sixteen filter channels mapped to parts, a dirty flag per parameter group, and closed-form conversions for cutoff, resonance, type and input gain. The cutoff byte turns out to be linear in the coefficient over an 8:1 span rather than logarithmic in Hz, the part's resonance offset counts double, and resonance clamps at 116. `docs/ymp706_registers.md`, "The per-voice filter". What the chip does with a coefficient is still the unknown, but it is now one reading to calibrate instead of a whole invented curve.
- [ ] **Find where the filter EG reaches the chip.** `FUN_0000C36C` is called only from the parameter-change path and the effect type change, so the EG is not going out that way. Session 2's `locate` watched 0x01068F78 through three filter EG sweeps and nothing in it moved. **The retake's window shots settle the direction this item has to go:** the four 1 KB dumps show the staged cutoff word and resonance word exactly where `FUN_0000C36C`/`FUN_0000C3D0` put them (`0xC0D + 0xA9·cut` to the integer, table A to one LSB), so the parameter path works and only changes *on a parameter change* — while `coefficients.csv` shows nothing in the region moves with an EG sweep. The CPU does not stream the EG; VOP3-1 runs it on-chip, which its IRQN pin already suited. The engine's own filter-EG model stays, but it is now known to model the chip's EG rather than a value the CPU recomputes. The remaining question is not *where* the EG goes but *what law* the chip's EG follows — answerable only from the corner-frequency sweep of experiment 4, not from RAM.
- [x] **The EG bias controller destination defaulted to full bias.** Destination 35 writes one per-part value that each operator's own EG bias sense then scales. With no controller set assigned to it, `ctrl_eg_bias` returned 255, the same value an assigned set at full depth gives while its source sits at rest, and `EGBIAS[255]` is 255: a sense of +3 buried the operator 35 dB down. Every voice with a positive EG bias sense and no controller on 35 was wrong, which is most of them. Demo song 10 was the visible case, silent at peak 0.005; it now plays. Two regression cases moved with it and `tools/regress_ref.json` is regenerated.
- [ ] **The constants that rest on one dataset.** Three conclusions in two days were confidently written up with a residual quoted and then turned out to be wrong, and all three failed the same way: a model fitted against one recording until the error stopped falling, with no second recording that varied the thing the model was wrong about. `cal::FM_INDEX` read 4.0 off a sweep aligned one step early. `cal::FRMT_BW_KNEE` read 44 at note 60 and 27 at note 36 because the window is a time and the engine held it at a fraction of the period. The harmonic forms were given the formant's bandwidth law because bandwidth 0 was the only setting recorded for them, and the unit ignores bandwidth on those forms entirely. In each case the fit was fine and the experiment was not.
  - What is left standing on a single dataset, worst first. **The "1" forms' window and all1/all2's geometry**, which is what the skirt sweep left standing. The window law itself is now MEASURED and exact where the geometry is known: p doubles per skirt step and odd2 and res2 land inside 0.9 dB over all eight settings. odd1 and res1 take the same law and are 10 to 14 dB out, because no `sin^p` reproduces them at any exponent. all1 and all2 are 36 to 66 dB out for a different reason, a two-period grain construction whose group slides off the fundamental as the skirt opens. `WIN_SKIRT_FRMT` is FITTED rather than measured and the formant keeps 2.5 dB that no member of the `sin^p` family can remove. [docs/skirt.md](docs/skirt.md). **`cal::EG_HOLD_LAG`**, 8.1 ms with six milliseconds of scatter that is the 192.3 Hz tick. **`cal::FRMT_WL_MAX`**, a grain-slot cap that binds only below the resolution of either recording. **`NOISE_BASE_HZ`, `NOISE_OCT` and `NOISE_BW_POW`**, fitted at note 60 alone: the second note (`05b_unvoiced2`, note 36) landed 2026-09-20 and the level law is now measured at two fundamentals. The power law held (`NOISE_BW_POW` 0.5 keeps the trend); what was off is a single gain hand, `cal::NOISE_LEVEL` 0.836.
  - The cheap guard, and it is already in place: score spectrum shape and envelope shape as well as level, and read every width in hertz as well as in partials. The formant's units were invisible until the same bandwidth was measured at two fundamentals and the two numbers were written side by side.

- [ ] **Confirm the INFERRED constants.** rgwan recorded the whole capture set on 2026-09-18 and it has settled a first round. `docs/capture_0918.md` is the working; `captures/analysis/` holds the per-segment numbers.
  - **Measured and in the engine.** The output path's fixed gain, `cal::OUT_GAIN` 0.14992, agreed on to 0.005 dB by nine segments. The channel accumulator's hard clip, `cal::CHAN_CLIP`, recovered identically from four and eight stacked carriers and shown to sit ahead of the filter loop. The filter loop's flat insertion loss, `cal::FLT_LOSS`, at 9.93 dB then and 5.69 dB now: both filter files play at note 24 where the request set's own pan scaling throws the sound hard right, and measuring one channel against an engine whose pan law was shy hid 4.24 dB of it until 2026-09-19. And `LEVEL_DB`, now 0.376287 rather than the 0.3795 this recording gave: the 2026-09-19 sweep measures the level register two ways that agree on a halving every sixteen steps, and shows that a fit over the whole ladder here ran into the output path's own droop below -68 dB and read high. `../FS1R.unlock/captures/fs1r_capture_session2_results.md`. The engine's master soft limiter went with them, since the hardware has a hard clip and a DAC ceiling and nothing that shape; it had been bending the engine's own level ladders by 0.02 dB a step. Re-measured, the median level difference across the 21 files goes from 4.28 dB to 0.23 dB, the two filter files land at 0.02 dB, and the clip reproduces `stack-4` and `stack-8` to 0.04 dB.
  - **Measured and confirmed as they stood.** The EG rate model, `rate_secs`, to 0.6% over rates 16 to 33 across 38 measured rates. `EG_LEVEL_DB` 1.5, to 1.5039 over forty steps. `CARRIER_DB` 1.5, straight over sixteen steps. The frequency scale and the note table. The operator sum, which is plain addition below the clip, and the filter input gain, which is 1.000 dB per step.
  - **10_envelope2, the second envelope recording, 2026-09-19.** Four more things, and only two of them are the EG. The key code law is a saturating table rather than a line: ten key codes four apart bound it to one value each, it stops at +-13 at both ends, and nothing of the form `trunc((a * c0 + b) / c)` fits all ten, so the engine carries the table. A rising EG aims at the top of the scale and clamps at its target, not a little past the target: `attackto-70` climbs to a target 21 dB down as fast as a climb to full, where the engine took five times as long, which is six decibels at the moment any operator with L1 under 99 speaks. The pan key scaling was 28 % shy, measured off the sweep the request file turned out to contain. And correcting that pan uncovered the `FLT_LOSS` error above, two mistakes that had been cancelling since 0918. `docs/aeg.md` is the working; `10_envelope2` goes from 0.82 dB median level and 3.37 dB envelope shape to 0.47 and 0.81, and the two filter files to 0.01 and 0.00.
  - **The amplitude EG's shape, 2026-09-19.** The 0918 pass measured the rate table and stopped there, and everything the rates are used for was still a DX7 guess. Three were wrong. The attack's time constant is a sixteenth of a full traverse where `EG_ATTACK_K` said a quarter, and a rise starts 53 dB below full rather than at silence, which together left a rate-24 attack 45 dB under the unit's after 400 ms. The hold is half a traverse at its rate plus a fixed 11.4 ms where the engine held for a whole one. And the key rate scaling was wrong by 11 to 16 chip steps at every note on the keyboard, always too fast, which is between 7 and 16 times the decay time. (**Corrected 2026-09-19.** This first read "dead code, the clamp returned zero for every note", which is false and came from `docs/ymp706_registers.md` glossing register 0xC0 as note/3 + 10 where it is note/3 + 73. `docs/aeg.md` has the corrected account and the table.) The 24 `tscale` segments recover the chip's rate exactly and give the law. Over the hundred envelope segments the shape error drops from 5.45 dB rms to 0.58; on the demo the envelope correlation goes from 0.955 median and 0.750 worst to 0.971 and 0.879. `docs/aeg.md`.
  - **Measured, still modelled wrongly.** The unvoiced level against bandwidth, the formant level against bandwidth (which pins `FRMT_NORM` at 1, not 0), the filter cutoff byte's reach, and AM sensitivity. **Detune is done.** It was a curve, not 2 cents a step, and the engine carries the measured one since 2026-09-20; what is left is the sixteen steps the capture skipped and whether the control is a frequency offset or a ratio, which `11_detune` and `FS1R.unlock`'s `sweep.py detune` both ask. Each is written up with its numbers in `docs/capture_0918.md`. **The unvoiced level is now measured twice.** `05_unvoiced` flagged that the level follows bandwidth differently on the chip than the model; `05b_unvoiced2` (note 36, the complementary fundamental) pinned it: with `NOISE_BW_POW` at 0.5 the engine sat 1.56 dB loud across the sweep with the trend right, so the fix is one hand constant — `cal::NOISE_LEVEL` 0.836 — rather than a new power law. `tools/analyze_unvoiced2.py` reads the take and regresses if `NOISE_LEVEL` or `NOISE_BW_POW` drift.
- [x] **Settle what was read but not certain.** Five CPU-side spots were marked "assumed" because the firmware had been read but not far enough. All are now traced and corrected:
  - **The controller matrix** was wrong in five ways at once: the source bit order (PB, CAT and PAT sit at bits 6-8, with FC, BC, MC3, MW and MC4 after them, not at the end), the source values (the knobs and MIDI controls are bipolar `(v - 64) * 2`, not raw), the gain (`>> 6` with a bias on each positive operand, not `>> 6` of the raw product), the missing clamps, and the assumption of a single uniform scaler where the firmware has three and picks by destination. Destinations 17-33 edit a part byte rather than contributing an offset, exactly as the manual's "overwrites the edit buffer" note says.
  - **Frequency bias** (destination 36) was routed but never applied: `(int16)(scaled * sense * 0x10) >> 2` on the operator's frequency word.
  - **The part EG offsets** reach T1, T2, **T3** and T4, not T1, T2 and T4: FUN_00019414 walks the part's bytes 0x1A, 0x1B, 0x1B, 0x1C. The hold rate takes its +4 only when it is below 0x3F.
  - **The Fseq pitch offset** is confirmed exact, constant for constant, against FUN_000127FC. The formant tracking term that used to sit alongside it was an invention and is gone: nothing in the firmware shifts a frame's formant frequencies, which is the point of the format.
  - **MIDI clock** weights each tick by 25, 50, 100, 200 or 400 for speed words 0 to 4 (FUN_0001ACB6), so the 1/4 to 4/1 ratios were right; the free-running `VELW[adjust] * 84 * 1000 / ratio + 2884` is confirmed instruction for instruction.
- [x] **Check the formant operator against the firmware's own formula.** `tools/check_formant.py` builds a voice with one formant operator at a known setting, works out where TRANS, FRMDET and the ratio tables say that formant belongs, and measures where it lands across three octaves. The fundamental tracks the key to within 0.25 %, the formant stays put while it does, and transposing a 800 Hz formant up twelve semitones lands on the 1.6 kHz setting exactly. The tolerance widens with f0 / formant because a formant is only locatable to about half the harmonic spacing. This checks the frequency, which is read from the firmware; the window shape around it is still a model from the patent.
- [x] **Cover it in the regression.** The suite could not see any of the above: it never moved a controller and always played the Fseq's own assigned note. `-cc num=val` sends control changes before an offline render, and seven cases now bracket the controller matrix and the Fseq away from centre. The self check asserts the three scalers, the source conversions, the part gate and the global destinations.

- [ ] **The demo song, end to end.** The FS1R plays 15 demo songs out of its own EPROM, so the same byte stream drives the hardware and the engine and no capture rig is needed. `tools/extract_demo.py` decodes the player's event format (flash 0x0002E8B8) into `captures/demo/NN_Name.mid`, and `tools/check_demo.py` finds each render inside rgwan's recording of the whole demo and reports the alignment, the envelope correlation and the per-octave level difference. All 15 line up in order with about a second of reload pause between them. `tools/demo_probe.py` works one song at a time (its patch, the two takes side by side, one byte changed) and scores a whole build in one number, which is how the constants below were moved. The recording is a coarse reference: both sides carry effects, ours modelled and the unit's real, so a gain error in the effect model reads as a level offset in every song. What it has found, in the order it found it:
  - **The extracted MIDIs ran 4 % fast**, which is most of what held the envelope correlations down. The demo player's timer ISR sets its next compare from the running count rather than from a fixed period (flash 0x0002F28C), so every period carries the interrupt's own entry cost. The recording measures that at 4 %: song to song, the distance in rgwan's take is 1.040 times the nominal length, on all fifteen, with no reload gap left over. `tools/extract_demo.py` now writes a 520000 us quarter, 10.4 ms per delay unit.
  - **Song 10 "Accordion" was silent** in our render, peak 0.005 against a full-level original. Fixed: the EG bias controller destination defaulted to full bias instead of none (Tier 0 below). It now correlates 0.930 and its bands sit with the rest.
  - **Songs 4, 6 and 9 were 20 to 51 dB dark above 5 kHz.** The filter and the modulation index between them account for nearly all of it: Kalimba now sits inside 7 dB in every band, AN Superarp at -5.5 and -18.9 where it was -49 and -51, Fat Line at -5.4 and -24.1 where it was -21 and -43.
  - **The modulation index was a third of what the recording wants.** One cycle of phase deviation at full modulator level is the DX7-lineage guess; three is what the fifteen songs ask for. Mean absolute band error: 8.3 dB at one cycle, 6.9 at two, 6.3 at three, 6.4 at four. Raising the feedback alone to the same depth recovers only a third of it (7.7 dB), so it is the operator-to-operator index and not the feedback path. This is the single biggest calibration move so far. **Superseded by measurement, and then corrected.** The 2026-09-19 sideband sweep was read one step early and gave 4.0; anchored properly it gives **3.369**, and so does a second take. The analyzer had been lining each sweep up on where its tone begins, and `sweep.py` brings its note up 1.55 s before the first step writes anything, so every spectrum was scored against its neighbour's register value. A ladder is shift invariant in slope, so `LEVEL_DB` came out right and nothing looked wrong. `03_fm` settles it independently and was never being read: its 25 spectra sit 4.46 dB out in band shape at 4.0 and 0.25 dB at 3.369. `../FS1R.unlock/captures/fs1r_capture_session2_results.md` carries the correction where the 4.0 was published.
  - **`tools/analyze_capture.py --compare` scores spectrum shape now**, the octave bands with each segment's own level divided out, which is what caught the index above and what nothing had been reading. It put numbers on three open items at once: `04_formant` at 6.3, 7.5 and 17.2 dB, `05_unvoiced` at 3.8 and 6.6, and `06_filter` at 14.7 dB on both files, where those same two files matched to 0.00 dB in level.
  - **The spectral skirt, 2026-09-19.** The grain window is `sin^p` and the skirt **multiplies** p rather than stepping it: `p = 2 * 2^skirt` on all/odd/res and `2 * sqrt(2)^skirt` on the formant, where the engine had `2 * (skirt + 1)` for everything. The proof needs no fit. res2 puts its grain in exactly one period under a carrier on partial 9, high enough that no line folds back, so the lines it produces are the window's own Fourier coefficients, and a `sin^(2n)` window's coefficients are the binomial row: the unit gives 1, 2, 1 at skirt 0, 1, 4, 6, 4, 1 at skirt 1, and the sin^8 and sin^16 rows after that, 0.00 to 0.35 dB rms against the exact coefficients over skirts 0 to 5. The engine lands inside 0.9 dB over all eight settings of both. `04_formant_2` goes from 1.13 dB of level error and 3.12 dB of band error to 0.29 and 2.52, and `04_formant_3`'s band error from 1.03 to 0.73. **And it closes what separates all1 from all2, odd1 from odd2, res1 from res2**, which had stood as "identical at every setting measured": they are identical at skirt 0 and four to five times apart by skirt 7, and every segment of the capture set sat at skirt 0. `docs/skirt.md` is the working. On the demo the tilt goes the other way, 2.55 to 2.72 dB, which is the all-form geometry fault the change exposes rather than the law: the capture set is bit-exact and single-parameter, the demo has both effect blocks in the path and a level the engine is not trying to match, so where they disagree the capture set is the reference.
  - **The resonant forms and the register the band parameter really uses, 2026-09-19.** rgwan ran the band sweep and reported that only `frmt` responded, then that `res1` and `res2` responded once it was pointed at register 0x230 but the four harmonic forms still did not. Both observations were right and both are now measurements. **`res1` and `res2` put the peak on partial `byte + 1`**, at every one of a hundred values, on both forms, at two fundamentals, where the engine's curve topped out at 32 times the fundamental; and the group is three lines with the neighbours 6 dB down and nothing beyond, which is a `sin^2` window exactly one grain period long and is a far stronger constraint than a fit. `04_formant_3` goes from 3.05 dB of level error and 16.89 dB of spectrum error to 0.30 and 1.03, which was the biggest number outside the effects. Normalising the grain sum by its own window length then put all six non-formant forms inside 0.14 dB on one constant and retired an ad-hoc factor. **Voice byte 6 does nothing at all to all1, all2, odd1 and odd2**, which is correct behaviour and not a missed target: the byte carries two parameters, the formant's bandwidth at 0x218 and res1/res2's resonance at 0x230, and those four forms read neither. Their one shape control is the skirt, which rgwan confirmed from the front panel moves every waveform except sine. `FS1R.unlock`'s `sweep.py skirt` measures it by parameter change; that run had been sending a nine byte frame where every FS1R parameter change carries two data bytes, so the unit dropped all of them and the LCD read skirt 0 throughout. Fixed to go through `fs1r_patch.param`, which has built the ten byte form all along, and the run now reads byte 5 back off the unit before it records anything.
  - **Two capture scripts were writing to the wrong hardware, and reading the firmware settled both.** `sweep.py filtcoef` passed a coefficient *slot* to `FUN_0000B5E2` as if it were a VOP3 register. That routine stores at `0x00800200 + 2 * reg`, so slot 0x189 became `0x00800512`, outside the chip's 128-register window and on the same chip select as the front panel, which is why the LCD and the panel LEDs flashed through the whole sweep. The firmware ships a coefficient as a pair, register 0 for the slot and register 11 for the value, and the run does that now. `sweep.py keycode` fed event 0x181, whose handler `FUN_00026A72` takes no data at all: it zeroes three channel fields and returns. Two runs of it measured the note's own key code sixty-four times. Register 0xC0 has no event and no per-channel array, so the run is disabled with the reason in its own output.
  - **The formant window and the harmonic forms, 2026-09-19.** `06_filter`'s 14.7 dB was not the filter: both files drive the loop with an all1 operator, and the unit gives that one partial where the engine gave a full harmonic series. Reading `04_formant`'s seventy-five spectra settles three things. The bandwidth **does nothing at all below byte 44** and halves the window every 8 steps above it, where the engine opened from byte 0 and halved every 20; that alone takes the sweep from 11.9 dB of spectrum error to 2.0 and explains the 0918 note that the formant level is flat to bandwidth 40. The harmonic forms are the same windowed carrier the formant is, with the carrier at the operator's own frequency and the window from the bandwidth, not a fixed quarter-period pulse train at DC; the odd forms need their grains phase continuous or the group lands on the even partials. And `cal::FLT_LOSS` moves a third time, 9.93 to 5.69 to **1.23 dB**, because it had been absorbing the spectral form's error on top of the pan's. `docs/formant.md` is the working. What is left there is `res1`/`res2` at 16.9 dB, the skirt at 3.2, and a bandwidth sweep on the harmonic forms, which nothing in the capture set has.
  - **The top octave is the biggest thing left.** Above 10 kHz the engine is 10 to 45 dB dark on the worst songs. It is not a noise floor in the recording: measured against each song's own 100 Hz to 2 kHz band, the unit's 12 to 20 kHz sits anywhere from -9 dB (Fat Line) to -70 dB (Kalimba), so it is synthesis content and it tracks how hard the patch drives its operators. Raising the modulation index closed a good part of it, which is the same story. What is left is whatever else spreads energy up there: the formant window's skirt, the harmonic forms, and whatever the chip's own arithmetic adds.
  - **Song 1 "Vokodrone" was the worst of the fifteen, and tracing what it touches found three firmware bugs.** It is the demo's hardest case on purpose: four parts, an Fseq on part 4, and a foot controller and a mod wheel driving six controller sets for the whole song (1123 CC 4 messages and 901 CC 1). Isolating its parts against rgwan's recording put the fault in the engine rather than in a constant, and each one is read out of the firmware:
    - **An Fseq that had played out blasted its operators at full level.** The operators an Fseq drives had their own level forced to zero attenuation on the assumption that the frame would overwrite it, and when the sequence stopped advancing nothing did. The frame writer FUN_000197B4 is gated only on the mode byte and the part, not on the sequence still running, so the registers hold the last frame; only the start delay gates it, by clearing the track masks in FUN_000125F8 while DAT_010291CA counts. Song 1 was 19 dB loud over its closing vocal phrase.
    - **The Fseq retrigger test looked at every part.** "First note" is the Fseq part's own held-note count: FUN_0001228C increments DAT_010288DC[part] and calls the trigger only when it reaches 1. Ours scanned all 32 channels, so any other part holding a note stopped the sequence restarting and it ran on out of step.
    - **The frequency bias controller reached operators the firmware leaves alone, and then froze.** FUN_0001E838 adds the bias word only in its formant and fixed branches; the ratio branch adds nothing, and FUN_0001D056 zeroes the unvoiced one outside normal mode. Ours added it everywhere, so a mod wheel swept song 1's bass operators by up to an octave and a half that the hardware never moves. Worse, the whole controller matrix was read at note-on: the bias, both bandwidths and the EG bias were frozen for the life of the note, while the firmware re-runs the destination handler whenever a source moves and rebuilds the per-part arrays (FUN_0001B334 and FUN_0001D056). Song 1 holds a thirty-second chord under a moving foot controller.
    - And the sets do not compose. Every direct destination handler **stores** a per-part byte (ctrlDest_36 writes DAT_01029748[part], ctrlDest_37 DAT_01029758, ctrlDest_39 DAT_0102979A) and FUN_00014DDC walks the eight sets in order, so two sets on one destination overwrite rather than add, exactly as for the destinations that edit a part byte. `ctrl_offset` used to sum them.
  - **Songs 8 and 15 are 10 to 20 dB quiet across every band**, and both play the same vocal patch: eight unvoiced operators at bandwidth 7 or 8, half of them linkFF. Songs 1 and 13, whose unvoiced operators are wide (20 to 48) and loud, are 8 to 15 dB bright instead. So the noise formant's level follows its bandwidth differently on the chip than in our model. `cal::NOISE_BW_POW` is the knob for it, and moving it from 0.5 to 1.0 changes those songs by less than a decibel, which says the unvoiced operators are not what dominates them either. `05_unvoiced` in the capture set is the file that settles this.
  - **Full Tines lost four seconds of its tail, and it was the key rate scaling.** The song holds the sustain pedal down and the hardware rings for four seconds after the last note off where ours rang for one. It was never the pedal and never the rate curve: the engine's key rate scaling was wrong by 11 to 16 chip steps at every note, always too fast, which is 7 to 16 times the decay time. With the law measured off `02_envelope_3` the engine's last audible frame moves from 14.02 s to 17.57 s against the unit's 18.28, and the song's envelope correlation from 0.838 to 0.976, from the worst of the fifteen to above the median. 0.7 s of tail is left. `docs/aeg.md`.
  - **`captures/demo/render` was stale and `tools/demo_scores.txt` with it.** Fourteen of the fifteen files predated the engine and came out 3.9 to 14.4 dB quieter when re-rendered, so the 5.50 dB line described a build that no longer existed. Re-rendered 2026-09-19 and the log restarted from a measured before and after.
  - **The engine sits about 6 dB under the unit on the demo**, median over the fifteen songs, down from 13 as the filter loop's insertion loss has been re-measured twice on 2026-09-19 and stable on both sides of everything else. The capture set does not do this, the 0918 work put the 21 reference files inside 0.23 dB, so it is the recording rather than `namespace cal`: `FS1R DEMO.flac` is one take through whatever gain rgwan's converter sat at, where the capture set was measured off the digital tap. `tools/demo_probe.py score` now reports the tilt, each song's per-band error with its own median divided out, alongside the raw number. Rank builds on the tilt.
  - Current state: envelope correlation 0.819 to 0.992, median 0.978, from 0.750 and 0.955 before the EG work; mean band tilt 2.52 dB from 3.10, and 2.4, 1.5, 1.3, 1.6, 1.2, 1.5, 2.0, 4.0, 7.2 dB per band from 40 Hz to 10 kHz, so everything from 40 Hz to 2.5 kHz sits inside 2.4 dB of the unit. Ana-Unison is the one song the formant work moved the wrong way on correlation, 0.872 to 0.819 while its tilt went 4.26 to 2.90; `docs/formant.md` has it.

  - **The filter is a ladder, and the demo cannot tell.** `FUN_0000C3D0` sends two resonance coefficients per channel, not one: with `r = 2^(-raw/16)`, table 0x374B24 is `A = 1 - r` and 0x374C24 is `B = max(0, 0.5 - 2r^2)`, quadratic in the same damping and zeroed for HPF and BEF, and `FUN_0000CA44` gives only LPF18 and LPF12 a tap mix and a doubled input. One shared cutoff with a feedback term and an input-side term is a ladder, so the three lowpasses are now taps off one 4-pole cascade instead of three separate topologies, and the invented "second pole pair runs flat at 0.7" is gone. Against the recording it is a wash: 5.55 dB where the old model scored 5.50, and 4.67 against 4.60 over the eleven songs whose numbers move at all. Two settings inside it did get settled. Spending table B on the passband is wrong, it reads as a flat +4 dB across every band of the songs whose patches already sat near 0 dB, so `RESO_COMP` is 0; and the textbook self-oscillation point, `LADDER_K` 4, oversharpens the peak, 5.65 against 5.55 at 2 with the 5120 Hz band at +8.3.
